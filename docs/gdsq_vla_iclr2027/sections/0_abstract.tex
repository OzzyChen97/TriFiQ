\begin{abstract}
Post-training quantization (PTQ) of a vision-language-action (VLA) policy must preserve task success, not just tensor fidelity. Unlike a predictor evaluated on fixed inputs, a compressed policy can change its future observations through its actions. This feedback motivates evaluating complete precision configurations and conditioning activation quantization on realized inputs. We introduce \method, a training-free framework combining exact-byte precision adaptation with input-conditioned 8-bit activation ranges. An action-weighted representation-stability allocator supplies the initial mask; a complete-policy deviation audit enforces conservative acceptance of refinements. Across 50 RoboCasa365 tasks and 2,500 episodes per headline configuration, GR00T reaches \textbf{54.0\%} success at 2.22$\times$ static compression, compared with 30.4\% for size-matched QuantVLA and 55.1\% for FP16. On $\pi_{0.5}$, adapting an over-budget initializer yields 27.7\% success at 2.70$\times$ compression, versus 26.2\% for FP16. A matched GR00T intervention isolates activation-range construction: input-conditioned ranges obtain 269/500 successes versus 0/500 for the registered frozen-range control. A compression sweep and a 4,000-episode LIBERO comparison establish both transfer and limits: more FP16 layers need not yield higher success, and deviation-based acceptance does not establish success optimality. These results identify deployment quantization as a consequential part of closed-loop policy compression, while separating storage savings from runtime acceleration.
\end{abstract}
