\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\paragraph{Overview.}
\method converts a frozen FP16 VLA into one static W4/FP16 policy while accounting for the data distribution induced by that policy. Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline} organizes the method around three contexts that conventional fixed-data PTQ omits. Temporal context defines behavioral fidelity over composed physical action sequences; whole-policy context gives final authority to an abstaining, exact-budget policy audit rather than a layer ranking; and deployment context conditions A8 ranges on the current input. The output is one frozen mask and one activation rule, without task routing or output correction.

\subsection{Problem Setting and Design Constraints}
\label{sec:problem}

Let $f_{\theta}(o,\epsilon)$ denote a frozen FP16 VLA that maps observation $o$ and generative noise $\epsilon$ to an action chunk $A\in\mathbb{R}^{H\times d}$. The target Linear layers are indexed by $\mathcal{L}$. A mask $M\in\{4,16\}^{|\mathcal{L}|}$ chooses signed-nibble W4 or native FP16 for every target, and $Q_M(f_\theta)$ denotes the instantiated mixed-precision policy. Let $\mathcal D_M$ be the closed-loop distribution over observation and replan sequences induced when $Q_M(f_\theta)$ executes its own actions. The conceptual full-space target is
\begin{equation}
 M^\star=\arg\min_{M\in\{4,16\}^{|\mathcal L|}}
 \mathbb E_{\tau\sim\mathcal D_M}\!\left[
 D_{\mathrm{ctrl}}\!\left(Q_M(f_\theta),f_\theta;\tau\right)\right]
 \quad\text{s.t.}\quad C(M)\le C_{\max},
 \label{eq:problem}
\end{equation}
where $D_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$ compares the quantized and teacher policies in physical control space on sequence $\tau$, and $C(M)$ is exact packed static-component storage. Unlike fixed-buffer PTQ, Equation~\ref{eq:problem} makes the evaluation distribution depend on the precision plan. Exhaustively collecting $\mathcal D_M$ for every mask is infeasible. We therefore use teacher-induced contexts $\mathcal D_T$ for low-cost screening, then collect candidate-induced contexts $\mathcal D_M$ only for shortlisted complete policies. Section~\ref{sec:fcp} defines the finite class $\mathcal C(M_0)$ that is actually audited and the abstaining output $\widehat M$.

This distribution dependence follows from ordinary closed-loop error propagation. For a local linearization with action error $e_t=a_t^Q-a_t^T$,
\begin{equation}
 \delta x_{t+1}=A_t\delta x_t+B_te_t,
 \qquad
 \delta x_T=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\Phi_{T,t+1}B_te_t,
 \label{eq:closed_loop_propagation}
\end{equation}
where $\Phi_{T,t+1}$ composes the intervening state Jacobians. Per-step MSE measures $\lVert e_t\rVert^2$ but ignores propagation, coherent error direction, and the observations reached after the deviation. This motivates a sequence-level control contract and candidate-state audit; it is not, by itself, evidence that a particular surrogate predicts task success best.

The full-policy audit is label-free: success and reward never enter $D_{\mathrm{ctrl}}$. Task identifiers only stratify uncertainty estimates, selection noise is paired, and a second noise stream is reserved for post-freeze audit. The complete initializer pipeline is not label-free because a four-task success sweep chooses the upstream CKA/CS ratio. The output is one mask and one activation rule shared by every task and observation. No gradient update, task-conditioned routing, output correction, or FP16 bypass is permitted.

These constraints separate compression design from test-time adaptation. A candidate may exploit the FP16 teacher and unlabeled on-policy observations before deployment, but it cannot observe task success or change its precision state online. This makes a selected mask auditable as a fixed model artifact and prevents a favorable task mixture from being encoded as a runtime selector.

\subsection{Temporal Context: A Physical-Sequence Contract}
\label{sec:dpac}

\dpac defines what behavioral preservation means for a chunked, replanning VLA policy. It moves the fidelity criterion from tensor reconstruction to temporally composed physical control; the current evidence uses it as a specification and acceptance statistic rather than claiming proven predictive superiority over MSE or prior surrogates.

\paragraph{Paired physical actions.}
For task--seed sequence $u$, let $A^P_{u,r,h}$ be the final action from policy $P\in\{T,Q\}$ at retained replan $r$ and chunk index $h$. Both policies receive the same observation and initial action noise. We sort unique replan indices, inverse-normalize the final action chunks, and retain the complete $H=16$ actions executed before the next replan. Thus a sequence with $R_u$ retained replans contains $N_u=16R_u$ ordered actions in $d=12$ deployed coordinates, without overlapping forecasts or deduplication. Only complete, finite chunks recorded before rollout termination enter the buffer. For coordinate $k$, a single global FP16-only scale is
\begin{equation}
 \tilde{s}_k=\max\!\left(1.4826\operatorname{MAD}_k,0.1\operatorname{RMS}_k\right),\qquad
 s_k=\max\!\left(\tilde{s}_k,0.05\operatorname{median}_{j}\tilde{s}_j,10^{-4}\right).
 \label{eq:global_scale}
\end{equation}
It is estimated once over the entire selection buffer and is never re-estimated per task or sequence. This prevents a severely corrupted candidate from making its own error appear small by inflating the denominator.

\paragraph{Local and accumulated error.}
Flatten sequence $u$ in control-time order and define $z_{u,t}=(a^Q_{u,t}-a^T_{u,t})/s$. We use the exactly-zero Charbonnier penalty $\rho(z)=2(\sqrt{1+z^2}-1)$. The sequence-level local and prefix terms are
\begin{equation}
 d_{\mathrm{local}}^{(u)}=\frac{1}{N_ud}\sum_{t,k}\rho(z_{u,t,k}),\qquad
 d_{\mathrm{prefix}}^{(u)}=\frac{1}{N_ud}\sum_{t,k}
 \rho\!\left(\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{t}z_{u,j,k}}{\sqrt{t}}\right).
 \label{eq:local_prefix}
\end{equation}
The $\sqrt{t}$ normalization preserves sensitivity to coherent bias. Alternating errors can cancel, whereas a small error with constant sign remains visible as its prefix grows. This distinction is important for action chunks because consistent translation or rotation bias can move the end effector away from the teacher trajectory even when per-step MSE is small.

\paragraph{Control-specific components.}
Three terms capture deviations that coordinatewise residuals miss. The pose term right-composes translation and rotation-vector increments in SE(3) and measures normalized teacher--candidate trajectory drift. The stitch term compares changes in the boundary jump between consecutive replans, while the gripper term compares both softened command state and event timing. Appendix~\ref{sec:metric_specification} gives their exact formulas, action semantics, edge cases, and evaluation order.

The five normalized components receive equal weight:
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 d_{\mathrm{PAC}}^{(u)}&=\tfrac{1}{5}\bigl(
 d_{\mathrm{local}}+d_{\mathrm{prefix}}+d_{\mathrm{pose}}+
 d_{\mathrm{stitch}}+d_{\mathrm{grip}}\bigr),\\
 \dpac&=\operatorname{mean}_u d_{\mathrm{PAC}}^{(u)}+
 \operatorname{CVaR}_{0.9,u}\!\left(d_{\mathrm{PAC}}^{(u)}\right).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:dpac_sequence}
\end{equation}
Here $u$ is a task--seed sequence of ordered replans. The outer CVaR gives the worst ten percent of sequences the same total weight as the mean, exposing rare catastrophic deviations without duplicating an observation-level tail term.

We retain \dfunc as a complementary local final/kinematic diagnostic on the same physical chunks; it is not a second closed-loop loss. Its exact specification is deferred to Appendix~\ref{sec:metric_specification}.

\subsection{Whole-Policy Context: An Abstaining Precision Audit}
\label{sec:fcp}

Mixed-precision selection is treated as a statistical acceptance problem, not merely a ranking problem. Layerwise evidence can propose a precision plan, but only the instantiated complete policy can accept it. When no candidate clears the declared evidence threshold, the audit abstains by retaining its initializer $M_0$.

\paragraph{Calibration-derived initialization.}
The audit starts from a frozen seed mask $M_0$. A predeclared CKA/CS initializer scores layerwise W4 interventions, applies action-impact and RMS/saturation guards, and emits byte-feasible complete masks. A four-task development sweep selects the fixed proxy ratio; its 100-W4/16-FP16 mask is frozen before the full-context search and formal evaluation. We audit this initializer once, without post-hoc restarts.

\paragraph{Context buffer.}
We collect FP16 on-policy observations from 12 tasks, with four each from Atomic-Seen, Composite-Seen, and Composite-Unseen. Three seeds per task and four replans per sequence yield 36 sequences and 144 observations. A hash of task and seed assigns early, middle, or late rollout windows, preventing the selector from seeing only easy initial states. Task identifiers stratify statistics, but the result remains one global mask.

\paragraph{Coordinate counterfactuals.}
Starting from $M_0$, round one flips every W4 target to FP16 individually. Round two flips every target state around the round-one winner. Each intervention is instantiated as a real mixed-precision network and compared with its complete-mask background. The resulting conservative benefits feed an exact sparse Pareto 0--1 dynamic program under
\begin{equation}
 C(M)=C_{\mathrm{fixed}}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{L}}C_i(M_i)
 \le 1.10\,C_{\mathrm{QuantVLA}}.
 \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
Layer count is not a constraint because two layers can have different tensor and metadata sizes. The dynamic program is only a candidate generator. Additive counterfactual benefits cannot represent interactions between simultaneous layer changes.

Let $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{DP}}(M_0)$ denote the masks emitted by this additive program and $\mathcal C_{\mathrm{struct}}(M_0)$ the five predeclared structured proposals. The implemented candidate class is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal C(M_0)=\{M_0\}\cup\mathcal C_{\mathrm{DP}}(M_0)\cup
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{struct}}(M_0),
\label{eq:candidate_class}
\end{equation}
not the exponential domain in Equation~\ref{eq:problem}. Complete-policy evaluation can detect interactions only for masks that enter $\mathcal C(M_0)$.

\paragraph{Task-level minimax adjudication.}
For metric $q\in\{\dfunc,\dpac\}$ and task cluster $g\in\{\mathrm{all},\mathrm{Atomic},\mathrm{C\text{-}Seen},\mathrm{C\text{-}Unseen}\}$, let $\Delta_{q,t}(M)$ be the paired candidate-minus-$M_0$ task scalar. We define
\begin{equation}
 U_{q,g}(M)=
 \frac{\operatorname{mean}_{t\in g}\Delta_{q,t}(M)+
 \operatorname{SE}^{\mathrm{jack}}_{t\in g}[\Delta_{q,t}(M)]}
 {\operatorname{mean}_{t\in g}q_t(M_{\mathrm{base}})},
 \qquad
 J(M)=\max_{q,g}U_{q,g}(M).
 \label{eq:minimax}
\end{equation}
The leave-one-task-out jackknife makes the task, rather than an observation, the uncertainty unit. A proposal is eligible only if $J(M)<0$ and the unnormalized one-SE upper bounds for pose, stitch, and gripper are all non-positive. These are conservative control-deviation guards, not physical-safety certificates.

\paragraph{Candidate-state check and freeze.}
Teacher-state observations can underrepresent the states induced by a changed policy. We therefore regenerate four to eight replans for the three best eligible masks and define $J_{\mathrm{final}}=\max(J_{\mathrm{teacher}},J_{\mathrm{candidate}})$. With the same component guards, the frozen output is
\begin{equation}
\widehat M=\begin{cases}
\arg\min_{M\in\mathcal E(M_0)}J_{\mathrm{final}}(M),&\mathcal E(M_0)\ne\varnothing,\\
M_0,&\mathcal E(M_0)=\varnothing,
\end{cases}
\label{eq:restricted_freeze}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal E(M_0)\subseteq\mathcal C(M_0)$ contains byte-feasible proposals with $J_{\mathrm{final}}<0$ and non-positive pose, stitch, and gripper upper bounds. Ties favor smaller bytes, fewer FP16 layers, then lexicographic mask order.

\subsection{Deployment Context: Input-Conditioned Activation Quantization}
\label{sec:deployment}

Deployment context removes the assumption that a frozen calibration table covers the inputs later encountered by a closed-loop generator. The novelty claimed here is not a new rounding primitive: Hessian-aware W4 is the weight substrate, while the activation rule conditions its range on the current deployment input.

\paragraph{Weight quantization.}
For each W4 layer, 256 calibration observations form FP16 input second-order statistics $X^\top X$. A GPTQ-style sequential error-feedback pass selects clipping and quantizes signed weights in groups of 64~\cite{frantar2022gptq}. Two signed four-bit codes are packed per byte. Per-group scales and packing metadata are retained, while the FP16 weight tensor is not. Row rotation is the identity, so the reported method does not depend on an unreported learned transform.

\paragraph{Input-conditioned activation range.}
Even a calibrated static activation table can become mismatched as observation, flow step, and policy-induced state change. Our static counterfactual uses independently calibrated 99.9th-percentile scales for each target layer, input channel, and applicable flow step. \dyrange removes this lookup and recomputes a scale for every target Linear, forward call, and input channel:
\begin{equation}
 s_c=\max\!\left(\frac{\max |x_{\ldots,c}|}{127},10^{-6}\right),
 \qquad q_c=\operatorname{clip}\!\left(\operatorname{round}(x_c/s_c),-127,127\right).
 \label{eq:dyrange}
\end{equation}
The rule is deterministic and uses the input dtype. It has no calibration lookup, flow-step selector, task selector, or candidate branch. Equation~\ref{eq:dyrange} is applied uniformly to every input, and runtime correction is disabled.

\paragraph{Exact storage scope.}
We report the byte sum of fixed model components, packed W4 codes, per-group scales and metadata, and native FP16 tensors. Activations, CUDA workspaces, simulator state, and eager fake-quant caches are excluded. Under this common scope the final GR00T plan contains 100 W4 and 16 FP16 targets and occupies 962,068,480 bytes. The $\pi_{0.5}$ anchor contains 121 W4 and 59 FP16 targets and occupies 1,634,828,288 bytes. These values establish static compression only. They do not imply latency or peak-memory improvements.
