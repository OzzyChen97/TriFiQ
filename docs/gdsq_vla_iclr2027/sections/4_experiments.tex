\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Benchmark and models.}
We evaluate GR00T N1.5 and $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{intelligence2025pi05} on RoboCasa365 v1.0.1~\cite{nasiriany2026robocasa365}: 18 Atomic-Seen, 16 Composite-Seen, and 16 Composite-Unseen tasks. Each completed row uses 50 fresh scenarios per task and official horizons (2,500 episodes). All target-split rows use four flow steps and shared task--seed--replan noise; formal paired claims are restricted to registered significance families.

\paragraph{Configurations and storage.}
FP16 is the teacher; \quantvla W4A8~\cite{zhang2026quantvla}, Uniform W6, and $\Omega$-QVLA W4A4~\cite{wang2026omegaqvla} are low-bit references. The GR00T \method plan has 100 W4 and 16 native-FP16 layers without output correction. Exact static bytes include fixed components and packing metadata, but not activations, workspaces, simulator state, or fake-quant caches. Its 962,068,480 bytes are 0.998$\times$ the \quantvla cell and 2.22$\times$ smaller than FP16.

\paragraph{Statistics and evidence gate.}
We report task- and split-macro success and exact paired two-sided McNemar tests with Holm correction. The headline uses 50 seeds per task; component tests use seeds 0--9 on all 50 tasks. Frozen manifests bind plans, code, environments, coverage, and hashes; any failed gate suppresses the cell.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:main_results}

\paragraph{GR00T headline.}
\method obtains \textbf{54.0\%} success versus 30.4\% for \quantvla, a 23.6-point paired gain (Holm $p<10^{-4}$); it is higher/tied/lower on 48/1/1 tasks. FP16 obtains 55.1\% ($p=0.2929$ versus ours), which establishes neither equivalence nor superiority. Figure~\ref{fig:operating_points} shows the measured success--storage tradeoff and task-level consistency; it is not a total ordering.

\paragraph{$\pi_{0.5}$ transfer.}
Exact-budget pruning converts 41 protected $\pi_{0.5}$ layers to W4, giving 121/180 W4 layers, 1.523~GiB, and 2.702$\times$ compression. The four-flow-step policy records 693/2,500 successes: \textbf{27.7\%} overall and 59.6/15.4/4.3\% across the three groups. Its 1.6- and 2.9-point gaps over FP16 and \quantvla are descriptive; without a paired initializer comparison, this row isolates no audit gain.

\subsection{Evidence for the Full-Context Diagnosis}
\label{sec:attribution}

None of 116 flips or five structured masks passes the whole-policy rule, so the audit retains $M_0$; this shows neither selector gain nor global optimality.

\input{tables/core_rollout_plan}

Table~\ref{tab:core_rollout_plan} gives the matched 500-episode test: $M_0$ with \dyrange obtains 53.8\%, but the frozen table obtains 0.0\% (Holm $p=6.33\times10^{-81}$). This isolates failure of that static construction. Because the FP16-activation control is offline and compression excludes activations and workspaces, the result establishes neither A8 necessity nor runtime gains. Appendix~\ref{sec:dynamic_range_visualization} gives supporting diagnostics.

The Local-MSE and unchecked-proposal masks each obtain 53.0\% ($-0.8$ points; Holm $p=1.0$), establishing neither equivalence nor a separate \dpac/audit gain. Appendices~\ref{sec:component_rollout_detail} and~\ref{sec:fcp_visualization} give mask and paired details.

\paragraph{Protocol separation and deployment scope.}
Only the CKA/CS ratio uses outcomes on four retained development tasks. The subsequent 12-task audit sees paired FP16 actions but no success labels, after which the mask and analysis family are frozen. Eager fake quantization retains floating-point execution, so we claim no latency, live-memory, or physical-safety gain.
