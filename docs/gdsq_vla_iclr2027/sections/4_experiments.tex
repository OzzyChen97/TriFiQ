\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Models, tasks, and evaluation.}
We evaluate GR00T N1.5 and $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{bjorck2025groot,intelligence2025pi05} on RoboCasa365 v1.0.1~\cite{nasiriany2026robocasa365}: 18 Atomic-Seen, 16 Composite-Seen, and 16 Composite-Unseen tasks. Headline configurations use 50 seeds per task, the official horizon, four flow steps, and shared task--seed--replan noise (2,500 episodes each). Focused interventions use the same tasks with seeds 0--9. LIBERO tests transfer to four additional suites.

\paragraph{Comparators and storage.}
FP16 is the reference; QuantVLA W4A8~\cite{zhang2026quantvla}, Uniform W6, $\Omega$-QVLA W4A4~\cite{wang2026omegaqvla}, and local ActQuant/DA-PTQ adaptations provide compressed comparisons. W4 and A8 denote four-bit weights and eight-bit activations. Static storage includes fixed components, packed weights, scales, and metadata, but excludes activations and runtime workspaces. GR00T occupies 962,068,480 bytes, or 0.998$\times$ the QuantVLA footprint. The projected $\pi_{0.5}$ mask occupies 1,634,828,288 bytes and satisfies its registered ceiling; this is not an exactly size-matched comparison to every baseline.

\paragraph{Statistics and selection provenance.}
We report task/split-macro success and exact paired two-sided McNemar tests, with Holm correction within registered families. Inferential claims are restricted to those comparisons; non-significance does not establish equivalence or non-inferiority. A four-task development sweep selected the GR00T initializer weighting from seven choices, and these tasks remain in the headline. On the other 46 tasks, post-freeze success is 51.8/27.6/52.8\% for ours/QuantVLA/FP16. Frozen manifests bind configurations, code, coverage, and hashes; incomplete or invalid cells are suppressed.

\paragraph{GR00T: a size-matched behavioral contrast.}
\method reaches 54.0\% at 2.22$\times$ static compression, compared with 30.4\% for essentially size-matched QuantVLA and 55.1\% for FP16. The 23.6-point gain over QuantVLA is significant under the registered paired family (Holm $p<10^{-4}$); the comparison with FP16 gives $p=0.2929$. Thus the compressed policy approaches the observed reference success rate, without an equivalence claim. Its precision mask is unchanged by FCP, so this system-level gain cannot be attributed to selector-induced mask improvement.

\paragraph{$\pi_{0.5}$: cross-architecture budget adaptation.}
The feasible policy reaches 27.7\% at 2.702$\times$ compression, compared with 26.2\% for FP16 and 24.8\% for QuantVLA. It has the highest observed success among the displayed compressed configurations, whose storage and calibration protocols differ. The 1.5-point margin over FP16 is descriptive, not evidence that compression generally improves capability. Section~\ref{sec:pi05_fcp_main} isolates how the transferred precision configuration meets the budget.

\paragraph{Adapted-baseline scope.}
Local ActQuant reaches 52.9/25.2\% on GR00T/$\pi_{0.5}$ versus our 54.0/27.7\% (Holm $p=0.3102/0.0011$). Local DA-PTQ reaches 3.0/26.2\% ($p=0.0648$ on $\pi_{0.5}$). Both require architecture adapters and proxy calibration and are labeled source-protocol-nonequivalent. In particular, the low GR00T DA-PTQ result characterizes this adaptation, not the native method's performance on its original benchmark.

\subsection{Which Deployment Choices Change Behavior?}
\label{sec:core_ablation_main}

\paragraph{Post-allocation quantization at a fixed mask.}
GR00T permits a matched 100-W4/16-FP16 comparison. Replacing the initializer-stage DuQuant/static-A8 stack with Hessian-aware W4 and input-conditioned A8 raises success from 50.8\% to 54.0\% (370/289 paired wins/losses; Holm $p=0.0036$). Precision placement is identical, so this identifies a combined post-allocation effect rather than a gain from mask search.

\paragraph{Activation-range construction.}
Holding the mask and runtime fixed, the registered frozen-range control obtains 0/500 successes versus 269/500 for input-conditioned A8. All frozen-range episodes complete without a runtime or numerical failure. Independently, the measured median scale drift across applicable action-head layers is 10.8\%, 16.8\%, and 24.9\% at flow steps 2--4, with a worst layer--step value of 49.0\% (Appendix~\ref{sec:dynamic_range_visualization}). The intervention establishes the behavioral importance of range construction in this configuration. It does not isolate policy-induced state shift from calibration coverage or flow-step effects, nor establish failure of all static calibration schemes.

\paragraph{Precision count is not a monotonic predictor.}
A preregistered six-arm GR00T sweep holds the Hessian-g64/input-conditioned-A8 stack and layer ordering fixed. Success ranges from 51.4\% to 55.4\% while static compression spans 1.166$\times$--2.556$\times$ across 3,000 episodes. The all-W4 endpoint ties the least-compressed arm at 55.4\%. At 2,500-episode scale, the descriptive all-W4 endpoint reaches 52.8\% versus the anchor's 54.0\% (Table~\ref{tab:main_results}). These results do not establish that mixed precision is universally necessary, or that every extra protected layer improves behavior.

\subsection{What Does Complete-Policy Adaptation Establish?}
\label{sec:pi05_fcp_main}

\paragraph{Feasibility projection on $\pi_{0.5}$.}
The transferred initializer protects 100 of 180 targets and exceeds the byte ceiling by 205,587,047 bytes. FCP evaluates all 180 one-layer interventions in complete-policy context and converts 41 protected targets to W4, yielding a feasible 121-W4/59-FP16 anchor. Later proposals fail the guards, leaving this projected configuration deployed.

Appendix Table~\ref{tab:pi05_fcp_diagnostic} reports the paired mask diagnostic.

The registered 500-key diagnostic gives 28.2\% for the projected anchor versus 26.0\% for the initializer ($p=0.235$). The projection demonstrably enforces the budget; the observed success difference is positive but does not prove non-inferiority or an optimal choice of removals.

\paragraph{Retention on GR00T.}
The GR00T initializer already fits the budget. All six emitted proposals fail the teacher-state deviation screen, so FCP returns $M_0$ without candidate-state adjudication. These proposals retain one to five FP16 layers rather than the anchor's 16; the ``single-layer'' proposal is a one-protected-layer configuration, not a one-flip neighbor. A subsequent 3,000-episode diagnostic finds no Holm-significant success difference from the paired anchor (minimum adjusted $p=0.891$; Appendix~\ref{sec:fcp_visualization}). This records conservative proxy-based retention, not evidence that rejecting the proposals prevented task failure or exhausted local improvements.

\subsection{How Much Do Offline Proxies Explain?}

\paragraph{Initializer signal ablation.}
At identical static bytes and with the same post-allocation stack, CS+CKA-DiT reaches 53.26\% on the Primary-46 slice, versus 51.52\% for CKA-DiT alone and 50.65\% for CS alone. The $+1.74$ and $+2.61$ point differences favor fusion descriptively, but both have Holm $p=0.5796$ (Appendix~\ref{sec:signal_ablation}). They do not establish a statistically resolved benefit of the fused signal.

\paragraph{Deviation is not success ranking.}
A separate 60-mask, exact-byte diagnostic tests offline proxy validity across six distances from $M_0$. Pooled Spearman correlations with success degradation are $-0.116$ for \dpac and $-0.028$ for MSE. Distance confounds this comparison; within-distance \dpac correlations are positive but imprecise, and it does not consistently outperform MSE or \dfunc (Appendix~\ref{sec:predictive_validity}). We therefore interpret the metric as a predeclared deviation guard, not a validated surrogate optimizer for closed-loop success.
