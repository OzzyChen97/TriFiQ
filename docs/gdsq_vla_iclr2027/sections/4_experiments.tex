\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Benchmark and models.}
We evaluate GR00T N1.5 and $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{intelligence2025pi05} on the RoboCasa365 v1.0.1 target split~\cite{nasiriany2026robocasa365}: 18 Atomic-Seen, 16 Composite-Seen, and 16 Composite-Unseen tasks. Formal GR00T configurations use 50 paired seeds per task, official horizons, a fresh rendered environment, four flow steps, and 16 executed actions per replan. Initial action noise is deterministically keyed by task, seed, and replan, producing 2,500 paired episodes per configuration.

\paragraph{Configurations and storage.}
FP16 is the teacher and QuantVLA W4A8~\cite{zhang2026quantvla} is the uniform low-bit reference. We retain the previous GDSQ-VLA result to isolate the new common runtime. \method uses 100 W4 and 16 native-FP16 target layers for GR00T, with no correction. Storage is exact total static model-component bytes, including fixed components and packing metadata but excluding activations, workspaces, simulator state, and fake-quant caches. The GR00T plan occupies 962,068,480 bytes, or 0.998$\times$ the QuantVLA cell and 2.22$\times$ compression relative to FP16.

\paragraph{Statistics and evidence gate.}
We report unweighted task-macro success, split macro success, exact paired two-sided McNemar tests, and a 10,000-draw task-then-seed hierarchical bootstrap. The three preregistered comparisons against GDSQ-VLA, FP16, and QuantVLA receive Holm correction. Frozen manifests bind plans, code, environments, tasks, seeds, and source hashes. A paper cell is generated only when exact coverage and artifact hashes pass the local audit.

\method obtains \textbf{54.0\%} success, with 74.7\%, 43.9\%, and 40.9\% on Atomic-Seen, Composite-Seen, and Composite-Unseen. QuantVLA obtains 30.4\%; ours improves by 23.6 points with interval $[19.12,28.00]$ and Holm-adjusted $p<10^{-4}$.

\subsection{Where Does the Gain Come From?}
\label{sec:attribution}

The full-context search returns the historical 100-W4/16-FP16 mask unchanged, so it cannot explain the 3.2-point gain by a new layer allocation. Instead, the controlled difference is the common deployment substrate: group-64 Hessian W4 and dynamic A8, with all corrections disabled. On five development tasks and ten new seeds, this runtime obtains 31 successes versus 25 for the historical runtime (seven paired wins, one loss). Separately, static A8 is strongly refuted by the offline paired objective (467), whereas \dyrange is close to the FP16 activation control (0.52). These diagnostics support range adaptation as the principal implementation change, while \fcp supplies evidence that changing the GR00T mask is unwarranted in the tested neighborhood.

This distinction is central to our claim: \method is the complete validated mask plus the Hessian-W4/\dyrange runtime. We do not attribute the GR00T gain to discovering a new mask, and the local-optimum result does not prove global optimality outside the searched counterfactual family.

\subsection{$\pi_{0.5}$ Compression Boundary}
\label{sec:pi05}

The $\pi_{0.5}$ plan increases W4 coverage from 80/180 to 121/180 layers, reducing exact static bytes to 0.886$\times$ the previous main plan and reaching 2.702$\times$ compression relative to FP16. In the combined 100-episode screen, however, the compressed candidate obtains 29 successes versus 33 for the prior plan (seven paired wins, eleven losses). We register this result as a compression anchor and make no success-rate improvement, equivalence, or formal non-inferiority claim.

\paragraph{Deployment scope.}
