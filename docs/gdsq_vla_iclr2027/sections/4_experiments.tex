\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Benchmark and models.}
We evaluate GR00T N1.5 and $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{intelligence2025pi05} on the RoboCasa365 v1.0.1 target split~\cite{nasiriany2026robocasa365}: 18 Atomic-Seen, 16 Composite-Seen, and 16 Composite-Unseen tasks. Formal GR00T configurations use 50 paired seeds per task, official horizons, a fresh rendered environment, four flow steps, and 16 executed actions per replan. Initial action noise is deterministically keyed by task, seed, and replan, producing 2,500 paired episodes per configuration.

\paragraph{Configurations and storage.}
FP16 is the teacher and QuantVLA W4A8~\cite{zhang2026quantvla} is the uniform low-bit reference. \method uses 100 W4 and 16 native-FP16 target layers for GR00T, with no correction. Storage is exact total static model-component bytes, including fixed components and packing metadata but excluding activations, workspaces, simulator state, and fake-quant caches. The GR00T plan occupies 962,068,480 bytes, or 0.998$\times$ the QuantVLA cell and 2.22$\times$ compression relative to FP16.

\paragraph{Statistics and evidence gate.}
We report unweighted task-macro success, split macro success, exact paired two-sided McNemar tests, and a 10,000-draw task-then-seed hierarchical bootstrap. Reported baseline comparisons use the stored Holm-adjusted values from the preregistered family. Frozen manifests bind plans, code, environments, tasks, seeds, and source hashes. A paper cell is generated only when exact coverage and artifact hashes pass the local audit.

\label{sec:main_results}

\method obtains \textbf{54.0\%} success, with 74.7\%, 43.9\%, and 40.9\% on Atomic-Seen, Composite-Seen, and Composite-Unseen. QuantVLA obtains 30.4\%; ours improves by 23.6 points. At the episode level there are 721 paired wins and 132 losses; the exact McNemar $p$-value is $6.30\times10^{-99}$ and the Holm-adjusted value is $1.89\times10^{-98}$. The hierarchical 95\% interval for the gain is $[19.12,28.00]$ points.

FP16 obtains 55.1\%. Ours has 292 wins and 319 losses, with $p=0.2929$ and a 95\% interval of $[-3.88,1.76]$ points. We therefore report a non-significant difference from FP16, not equivalence or superiority.

\subsection{$\pi_{0.5}$ Compression Boundary}
\label{sec:pi05}

The $\pi_{0.5}$ plan increases W4 coverage from 80/180 to 121/180 layers, reducing exact static bytes to 0.886$\times$ the 80-W4 reference plan and reaching 2.702$\times$ compression relative to FP16. In the combined 100-episode screen, however, the compressed candidate obtains 29 successes versus 33 for the reference plan (seven paired wins, eleven losses). We register this result as a compression anchor and make no success-rate improvement, equivalence, or formal non-inferiority claim.

\paragraph{Deployment scope.}
The current implementation validates numerical behavior and exact packed storage accounting. Its eager fake-quant path retains floating-point execution, so the paper makes no end-to-end latency or live-memory claim. Fused low-bit kernels and real-robot evaluation remain future work.
