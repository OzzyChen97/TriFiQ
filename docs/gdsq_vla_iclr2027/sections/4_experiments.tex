\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Benchmark and models.}
We evaluate GR00T N1.5 and $\pi_{0.5}$~\cite{intelligence2025pi05} on the RoboCasa365 v1.0.1 target split~\cite{nasiriany2026robocasa365}: 18 Atomic-Seen, 16 Composite-Seen, and 16 Composite-Unseen tasks. Formal configurations use 50 paired scenarios per task, official horizons, and a fresh rendered environment, producing 2,500 episodes per configuration. GR00T uses four flow steps and executes 16 actions per replan. Initial action noise is deterministically keyed by task, seed, and replan, so paired policies see the same stochastic input.

\paragraph{Configurations and storage.}
FP16 is the teacher and QuantVLA W4A8~\cite{zhang2026quantvla} is the uniform low-bit reference. Uniform W6 isolates the effect of allocating more bits uniformly, while $\Omega$-QVLA~\cite{wang2026omegaqvla} represents a more aggressive W4A4 operating point. \method uses 100 W4 and 16 native-FP16 target layers for GR00T, with no output correction. Storage is exact total static model-component bytes, including fixed components and packing metadata but excluding activations, workspaces, simulator state, and fake-quant caches. The GR00T plan occupies 962,068,480 bytes, or 0.998$\times$ the QuantVLA cell and 2.22$\times$ compression relative to FP16.

\paragraph{Statistics and evidence gate.}
We report unweighted task-macro success, split macro success, exact paired two-sided McNemar tests, and a 10,000-draw task-then-seed hierarchical bootstrap. Reported baseline comparisons use stored Holm-adjusted values from the preregistered family. Frozen manifests bind plans, code, environments, tasks, seeds, and source hashes. A paper cell is generated only when exact coverage and artifact hashes pass the local audit; an absent or incomplete result remains ``--'' rather than being imputed.

\subsection{Main Results}
\label{sec:main_results}

\paragraph{GR00T headline.}
\method obtains \textbf{54.0\%} success, with 74.7\%, 43.9\%, and 40.9\% on Atomic-Seen, Composite-Seen, and Composite-Unseen. QuantVLA obtains 30.4\%; ours improves by 23.6 points. At the episode level there are 721 paired wins and 132 losses; the exact McNemar $p$-value is $6.30\times10^{-99}$ and the Holm-adjusted value is $1.89\times10^{-98}$. The hierarchical 95\% interval for the gain is $[19.12,28.00]$ points. Ours exceeds QuantVLA on 48 of 50 tasks, ties on one, and is lower on one, showing that the aggregate gain is not produced by a small set of outliers.

FP16 obtains 55.1\%. Ours has 292 wins and 319 losses, with $p=0.2929$ and a 95\% interval of $[-3.88,1.76]$ points. The split differences are $-0.9$, $+2.0$, and $-4.4$ points for Atomic-Seen, Composite-Seen, and Composite-Unseen, respectively; at task level ours is higher on 18, tied on six, and lower on 26. We therefore report a non-significant overall difference from FP16, not equivalence or superiority.

\paragraph{Task complexity and storage.}
Relative to QuantVLA, the gains are 25.1, 24.3, and 21.1 points across the three task groups. The persistence of the gain on Composite-Unseen is relevant to the method hypothesis because held-out task compositions provide a stress test for accumulated control error. This is an empirical pattern rather than proof that any individual \dpac component causes the gain. The storage comparison adds a separate constraint: ours is slightly smaller than the QuantVLA cell, whereas Uniform W6 reaches 51.7\% at 1.109~GiB. $\Omega$-QVLA is smaller at 0.599~GiB but obtains 38.7\%; thus Table~\ref{tab:main_results} should be read as a success--storage set of operating points rather than a single ranking.

\paragraph{$\pi_{0.5}$ boundary.}
The lower block of Table~\ref{tab:main_results} adds formal $\pi_{0.5}$ baselines, each evaluated on the 2,500-episode target split. Our $\pi_{0.5}$ row has a deliberately different role: exact-budget pruning increases W4 coverage from 80/180 to 121/180 layers, reduces static bytes to 0.886$\times$ the 80-W4 reference plan, and reaches 2.702$\times$ compression relative to FP16. It was screened only on two ten-seed waves over five tasks. The candidate obtains 29/100 successes versus 33/100 for the 80-W4 reference, with seven paired wins and eleven losses. Because the task and seed coverage differs from the formal rows, the 29/100 cell is a compression anchor only and is not compared with their success percentages.

These rollout cells remain pending and are not used to support the present claims. Lower-priority metric, search, and budget diagnostics are specified in Appendix~\ref{sec:extended_ablations}.

\paragraph{Protocol separation and failure accounting.}
Mask selection and formal success evaluation use disjoint evidence: the former receives paired FP16 actions on an unlabeled 12-task context buffer, whereas the latter is read only after the mask, quantizers, and analysis family are frozen. Formal cells require all 50 tasks and 50 scenarios per task. A simulator crash, numerical failure, missing episode, or hash mismatch prevents a cell from entering the table; the audit never silently changes the denominator. This separation matters for a mixed-precision search because even indirect access to rollout success could turn task allocation into supervised model selection.

\paragraph{Deployment scope.}
The current implementation validates numerical behavior and exact packed storage accounting. Its eager fake-quant path retains floating-point execution, so the paper makes no end-to-end latency or live-memory claim. Fused low-bit kernels and real-robot evaluation remain future work.
