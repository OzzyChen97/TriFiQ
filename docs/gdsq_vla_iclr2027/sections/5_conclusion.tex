\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \method, a training-free mixed-precision framework that evaluates quantization in complete policy context. \dpac makes prefix accumulation, physical pose, action-chunk stitching, gripper timing, and tail risk visible to precision selection; \fcp adjudicates complete masks under an exact byte budget; and \dyrange adapts A8 scales on every forward call. The final deployment uses one static W4/FP16 mask, with no task routing or runtime correction.

On GR00T N1.5, \method reaches 54.0\% RoboCasa365 success at 2.22$\times$ static component compression. It significantly improves over GDSQ-VLA by 3.2 points and is statistically tied with the 55.1\% FP16 teacher. The result also clarifies mechanism: full-context search validates the existing GR00T mask as a local optimum, while the measured gain arises on the common Hessian-W4 + dynamic-A8 runtime.

Our evidence is bounded in three ways. The local search does not prove global mask optimality; the $\pi_{0.5}$ result is only a 100-episode compression anchor, not a success claim; and eager fake quantization does not establish latency or live-memory gains. Extending the full formal matrix to additional policy families, implementing fused INT4/INT8 kernels, and validating on real robots are the next steps.
