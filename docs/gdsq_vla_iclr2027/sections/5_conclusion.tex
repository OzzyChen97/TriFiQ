\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Quantizing a policy that influences its future inputs requires evaluating more than local tensor fidelity. \method combines exact-byte precision adaptation with input-conditioned activation quantization, yielding 54.0\% GR00T success at 2.22$\times$ static compression and 27.7\% on $\pi_{0.5}$ at 2.70$\times$. Fixed-mask interventions identify deployment range construction as consequential, while the non-monotonic compression sweep cautions against treating FP16 layer count as a proxy for behavioral quality.

The evidence bounds these results. The static-range intervention does not isolate every source of distribution shift. Deviation guards do not predict success reliably, and GR00T retains its initializer without candidate-state adjudication. Its finite candidate set establishes no optimality. Evaluation is in simulation, some baselines require non-equivalent adaptations, and the generic backend saves memory but increases latency and energy. The supported result is a practical success--storage tradeoff whose behavior depends on deployment quantization.
