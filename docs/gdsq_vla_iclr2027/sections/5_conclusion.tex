\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented \method, a training-free mixed-precision framework that evaluates quantization in complete policy context. \dpac makes prefix accumulation, physical pose, action-chunk stitching, gripper timing, and tail risk visible to precision selection; \fcp adjudicates complete masks under an exact byte budget; and \dyrange adapts A8 scales on every forward call. The final deployment uses one static W4/FP16 mask, with no task routing or runtime correction.

On GR00T N1.5, \method reaches 54.0\% RoboCasa365 success at 2.22$\times$ static component compression. It improves over QuantVLA W4A8 by 23.6 points, while its difference from the 55.1\% FP16 teacher is not significant. The mechanism diagnostics clarify what the evidence does and does not establish: dynamic ranges avoid the severe static-A8 mismatch, and full-context search validates the initialized GR00T mask as a local optimum in the tested neighborhood.

Our evidence is bounded in four ways. The local search does not prove global mask optimality; the core component-removal rollouts are specified but not yet complete; the $\pi_{0.5}$ result is only a 100-episode compression anchor, not a success claim; and eager fake quantization does not establish latency or live-memory gains. Completing the ablation matrix, extending the formal evaluation to additional policy families, implementing fused INT4/INT8 kernels, and validating on real robots are the next steps.
