\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Vision-language-action (VLA) models map visual observations and language instructions to robot actions~\citep{brohan2023rt2,octo2024,kim2024openvla,black2024pi0,intelligence2025pi05,bjorck2025groot}. Their multimodal backbones and iterative action generators make model storage a practical deployment constraint, and post-training quantization (PTQ) reduces this footprint without retraining~\citep{xiao2023smoothquant,lin2024duquant}. The target, however, is not a smaller network but a compact policy that still completes tasks, and this distinction breaks the assumption on which standard PTQ evaluation rests. Low-bit VLA deployment is not conventional model quantization: compression changes the policy, and the changed policy changes the observations it will encounter. A mask that lowers action accuracy under a fixed calibration buffer is therefore evaluated on observations the deployed policy may never see, and a mask that looks admissible under fixed-buffer statistics can still alter accumulated bias, replanning discontinuities, and discrete control transitions once the policy acts.

We formulate this as \emph{byte-constrained policy configuration under a mask-induced rollout distribution} (Equation~\ref{eq:problem}): choose the layer-wise precision mask that maximizes expected task success under an exact storage budget, where the expectation is taken over the rollout distribution that the mask itself induces. Three consequences follow. The decision variable is a complete configuration, not an independent set of tensors, so simultaneous layer changes need not combine additively. The objective is task success on a mask-induced distribution, so layer-level proxies are proposals rather than decisions. The input distribution also moves with the policy, so activation handling belongs to the fidelity problem.

These consequences motivate a \emph{proposal--admission separation principle}: representation-level evidence proposes where precision is valuable, but only complete-policy action evidence decides whether an assembled configuration is admissible. A favorable allocation score neither certifies a mixed-precision policy nor replaces evaluating the assembled model, and an admissible configuration may retain a byte-feasible anchor when no candidate qualifies. Admission also cannot rest on pointwise action error, because equal local error can induce different accumulated, replanning, and control-transition behavior.

We introduce \method, a PTQ framework implementing these stages. First, \emph{Representation-Integrity-aware Precision Allocation} (RIPA) combines relational and distributional representation damage, reweights it by action impact, and applies numerical guards and byte-constrained allocation. Second, \emph{Full-Context Precision Protection} (FCP) evaluates interventions in the complete mixed-precision policy. Its D-PAC defines temporal policy deviation through accumulated bias, replanning continuity, and gripper events; complementary deviation conditions govern whether a complete candidate can replace a byte-feasible anchor. Third, \dyrange executes the frozen layout with second-order four-bit weights (W4) and input-conditioned eight-bit activations (A8), removing closed-loop activation-range shift while keeping precision fixed. Dynamic behavior concerns activation scales, not precision routing or gradient fine-tuning.

Under the shared RoboCasa365 50-task protocol, \method attains the highest observed success among the evaluated PTQ methods on both backbones. GR00T N1.5 reaches 54.0\% success at 2.22$\times$ audited Linear-component compression, 23.6 percentage points above approximately size-matched QuantVLA and within 1.1 points of the 55.1\% FP16 reference. On $\pi_{0.5}$, it reaches 27.7\% at 2.70$\times$ compression, compared with 24.8\% for QuantVLA and 26.2\% for FP16; these configurations are not all storage-matched. A 4,000-episode LIBERO comparison evaluates cross-benchmark transfer, while controlled ablations test allocation, budget projection, and execution separately. In the strongest component-level control, which fixes the mask, the quantized weights, and the seeds, input-conditioned A8 raises success by 10.6 points over a pooled static-A8 calibration.

Our contributions are threefold.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\arabic*)]
    \item \textbf{Problem formulation and separation principle.} We formulate low-bit VLA deployment as byte-constrained policy configuration under a mask-induced rollout distribution, and argue for a proposal--admission separation in which layer-level evidence proposes precision while only complete-policy action evidence decides admission.
    \item \textbf{Proposal and admission machinery.} RIPA converts representation integrity and action impact into a guarded, byte-feasible layout; D-PAC defines temporal policy deviation, and FCP uses it with complementary conditions for budget projection and complete-candidate admission, retaining a feasible anchor when none qualifies.
    \item \textbf{Strong low-bit VLA results.} We demonstrate leading observed success among the evaluated RoboCasa365 PTQ baselines on two backbones, with high-compression operating points and LIBERO transfer, and assess allocation, configuration decisions, and activation-range construction separately.
\end{enumerate}
