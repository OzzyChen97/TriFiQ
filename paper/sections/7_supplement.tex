\subsection{RIPA Allocation Details and Statistic Provenance}
\label{sec:init_provenance}

We construct the GR00T initializer with RIPA before FCP. In the historical implementation notation, $S_i=(\lambda_g\bar d_i^{\mathrm{geom}}+\lambda_d\bar d_i^{\mathrm{dist}})/(\lambda_g+\lambda_d)$, where $\bar d_i^{\mathrm{geom}}=\bar d_i^{\mathrm{rel}}$ and $\bar d_i^{\mathrm{dist}}$ are jointly normalized versions of $1-\widehat{\mathrm{CKA}}_i$ and $\widehat D_{\mathrm{CS},i}$. Equation~\ref{eq:cscka_allocator} writes the same cost as $\mathcal I_i=w_iS_i$. Action-impact weights prioritize layers whose one-layer intervention changes physical actions, while RMS and saturation guards retain unstable layers in FP16. A greedy byte-constrained solver followed by fixed-size flips produces nine complete candidate masks. Paired adjudication on 16 calibration observations selects one mask for each weighting without success labels.

\paragraph{Normalization and action weights.}
For each statistic $z\in\{1-\widehat{\mathrm{CKA}},\widehat D_{\mathrm{CS}}\}$, let $\mathcal P$ contain all finite measured $(i,b)$ pairs in the frozen score bank. The selector computes $\bar z_{i,b}=(z_{i,b}-\min_{\mathcal P}z)/(\max_{\mathcal P}z-\min_{\mathcal P}z)$ independently for the two terms, setting a constant term to zero when its range is below $10^{-12}$. Missing terms are omitted and the available fusion weights renormalized; a target without a usable score remains FP16. The deployed score uses the W4 entries. The relational field is \texttt{cka\_dit} (action-generator output), whereas CS is measured at the intervened Linear output.

The recorded action-impact source is the one-layer functional deviation $d_i= D_{\mathrm{func},i}$, not raw MSE or D-PAC. With $n$ targets, missing $d_i$ is replaced by the mean of measured values; if none are measured or their sum is nonpositive, $w_i=1$. Otherwise,
\begin{equation}
 v_i=\frac{n d_i}{\sum_jd_j},\qquad c_i=\operatorname{clip}(v_i,0.5,2),\qquad
 w_i=\frac{n c_i}{\sum_jc_j}.
\end{equation}
The final renormalization preserves mean one but rescales the clipping endpoints. The frozen GR00T bank gives $w_i\in[0.76535,3.06141]$, not $[0.5,2]$. Its raw \dfunc values range from $5.17\times10^{-5}$ to $0.32739$.

\paragraph{Why the two damage views are complementary.}
For paired feature matrices, $Y=X+\mathbf{1}b^\top$ preserves centered relations while translating the distribution. Conversely, $Y=PX$ for a row permutation $P$ preserves the empirical distribution but generally disrupts input-corresponding relations. Each view is therefore blind to a transformation the other detects, which is why RIPA scores both.

\paragraph{Hard numerical protection.}
For reference/quantized pooled outputs $X,Y$, the RMS guard is $r_i=\operatorname{median}_c|\log((\operatorname{RMS}(Y_c)+10^{-6})/(\operatorname{RMS}(X_c)+10^{-6}))|$. The saturation guard is the fraction of elements of $Y$ exceeding $P_{99.9}(|X|)$ in magnitude. Thresholds are 1.5 times the nearest-rank 99th percentile of measured W4 guard values, unless supplied by the frozen bank. GR00T uses $\tau_{\mathrm{rms}}=0.1318639107$ and $\tau_{\mathrm{sat}}=0.0023243131$; exceeding either removes a layer's quantization options and retains it in FP16. The protected numerical outlier is language layer 2's MLP down-projection. These are feasibility cuts, not soft penalties.

\subsection{Exact Metric Specification and Edge Cases}
\label{sec:metric_specification}

\paragraph{Sequence construction and action semantics.}
Each metric unit is a task--seed sequence $u$ with $R_u$ unique replans. We sort replans by their recorded integer index; each contributes the complete inverse-normalized $(16,12)$ chunk executed before the next replan, so $N_u=16R_u$. We discard later unexecuted forecasts, do not overlap adjacent chunks, and do not deduplicate predictions. A sequence contains only complete chunks collected before success, termination, truncation, or the official horizon. Duplicate replans, any shape other than $(16,12)$, or a non-finite value fail the metric gate rather than being padded or imputed. Coordinates 1--3 encode end-effector translation, 4--6 a rotation vector, 7 gripper close, and 8--12 the deployed base/control commands. Equation~\ref{eq:global_scale} normalizes each coordinate separately.

For coordinate $k$, we estimate a robust scale once from the complete FP16 selection buffer and never recompute it per task, sequence, or candidate:
\begin{equation}
 \tilde{s}_k=\max\!\left(1.4826\operatorname{MAD}_k,0.1\operatorname{RMS}_k\right),\qquad
 s_k=\max\!\left(\tilde{s}_k,0.05\operatorname{median}_{j}\tilde{s}_j,10^{-4}\right).
 \label{eq:global_scale}
\end{equation}
With $z_{u,t}=(a^Q_{u,t}-a^T_{u,t})/s$ and $\rho(z)=2(\sqrt{1+z^2}-1)$, the local and accumulated terms are
\begin{equation}
 d_{\mathrm{local}}^{(u)}=\frac{1}{N_ud}\sum_{t,k}\rho(z_{u,t,k}),\qquad
 d_{\mathrm{prefix}}^{(u)}=\frac{1}{N_ud}\sum_{t,k}
 \rho\!\left(\frac{\sum_{j=1}^{t}z_{u,j,k}}{\sqrt{t}}\right).
 \label{eq:local_prefix}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Pose map.}
For $\xi=[v;\omega]$, let $\Omega=[\omega]_{\times}$, $\theta=\lVert\omega\rVert_2$, and
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{Exp}(\xi)
&=\begin{bmatrix}
I+A\Omega+B\Omega^2&(I+B\Omega+C\Omega^2)v\\
0&1
\end{bmatrix},\\
A&=\frac{\sin\theta}{\theta},\qquad
B=\frac{1-\cos\theta}{\theta^2},\qquad
C=\frac{\theta-\sin\theta}{\theta^3}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:se3_exp_detail}
\end{equation}
The implementation uses Taylor limits for $\theta<10^{-5}$. Right multiplication, $T_t=T_{t-1}\operatorname{Exp}(\xi_t)$, interprets each increment in the current end-effector frame. The logarithm uses the principal rotation angle and inverse left Jacobian $V^{-1}=I-\Omega/2+c\Omega^2$, where $c=1/12$ near zero and $c=\theta^{-2}-(1+\cos\theta)/(2\theta\sin\theta)$ otherwise. The three control-specific components are
\begin{align}
 e^{\mathrm{pose}}_t&=\frac{\operatorname{Diag}(s_{1:6})^{-1}
 \operatorname{Log}((T^T_t)^{-1}T^Q_t)}{\sqrt t},\qquad
 d_{\mathrm{pose}}^{(u)}=\overline\rho_t(e^{\mathrm{pose}}_t),
 \label{eq:pose}\\
 d_{\mathrm{stitch}}&=\overline{\rho}\!\left(
 \frac{(A^Q_{r+1,1}-A^Q_{r,H})-(A^T_{r+1,1}-A^T_{r,H})}{s}
 \right),
 \label{eq:stitch}\\
 d_{\mathrm{grip}}^{(u)}&=\tfrac12\left[
 \overline\rho_t(g^Q_t-g^T_t)+
 \overline\rho_{t>1}((g^Q_t-g^Q_{t-1})-(g^T_t-g^T_{t-1}))\right],
 \label{eq:gripper}
\end{align}
where $g^P_t=\sigma(4a^P_{t,7}/s_7)$. Translation and rotation are normalized coordinatewise before averaging.

\paragraph{Functional diagnostic.}
For physical final chunks indexed by context $i$, define
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 e_i&=\frac{\lVert A_i^Q-A_i^T\rVert_F^2}
 {\max(\lVert A_i^T\rVert_F^2,10^{-8})},\qquad
 d_{\mathrm{final}}=\frac{\lVert A^Q-A^T\rVert_F^2}
 {\max(\lVert A^T\rVert_F^2,10^{-8})},\\
 \dfunc&=d_{\mathrm{final}}+d_{\mathrm{kin}}+d_{\mathrm{grip}}
 +2\operatorname{CVaR}_{0.9,i}(e_i).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:dfunc_detail}
\end{equation}
$d_{\mathrm{kin}}$ is one half of the sum of analogous relative errors restricted to translation and rotation coordinates. The logged $d_{\mathrm{solver}}=\operatorname{mean}_i e_i$ is a separate summary. In Equation~\ref{eq:dfunc_detail}, $\operatorname{CVaR}_{0.9,i}(e_i)$ averages the context-level samples $e_i$ at or above their empirical 90th percentile, rather than applying CVaR to that scalar mean. The registered \dfunc gripper term is the fraction of adjacent solver states for which active gripper changes ($>10^{-4}$ in both policies) disagree in sign. Because the shared selection path records only final physical chunks and not the internal solver trajectory, this legacy \dfunc gripper term is zero on that path. Gripper deviation still enters \dpac through Equation~\ref{eq:gripper} and the explicit component guard.

\paragraph{Evaluation order.}
For each candidate, we (1) validate paired records, sort replans, and inverse-normalize final chunks; (2) compute the global 12-coordinate scale once from all FP16 selection chunks; (3) evaluate Equations~\ref{eq:local_prefix}, \ref{eq:pose}, \ref{eq:stitch}, and \ref{eq:gripper} for each task--seed sequence; (4) form the five-component mean and worst-10\% sequence mean in Equation~\ref{eq:dpac_sequence}; and (5) compute task-level candidate-minus-$M_0$ differences, the jackknife standard error in Equation~\ref{eq:task_jackknife}, $J$, and the pose/stitch/gripper upper bounds. We set $d_{\mathrm{stitch}}=0$ for a one-replan sequence and the gripper-difference term to zero for a one-action sequence. Acceptance requires strict $J_{\mathrm{final}}<0$, non-positive component upper bounds, and the exact byte constraint; equality fails closed.

For $n_g$ task scalars $x_t$ in cluster $g$, the implemented leave-one-task-out jackknife standard error is
\begin{equation}
 \operatorname{SE}^{\mathrm{jack}}(\bar{x})=
 \sqrt{\frac{\sum_{t\in g}(x_t-\bar{x})^2}{n_g(n_g-1)}},
 \label{eq:task_jackknife}
\end{equation}
which is algebraically identical to the usual sample standard error of the task mean.

\subsection{Independent Budget-Projection Comparison}
\label{sec:projection_comparison}

This completed study isolates the deletion rule in FCP's budget projection. The protocol fixes two backbones, two budgets each, and three deletion rules, plus one unmodified initializer per backbone: 14 configurations with 50 tasks and seeds 20000--20009, totaling 7,000 episodes. Masks are fixed before these rollouts and use subsets of frozen parent second-order quantization artifacts, without requantization or selection feedback from the new success results. Historical findings motivated the prospective comparison; this does not make the entire research process preregistered.

\paragraph{Matched budgets and controls.}
Full-policy-score deletion is compared with final-action-error deletion and largest-layer-first deletion. The former simple control uses saved relative squared error of final physical actions, not raw MSE. Both simple rules include subset-sum feasibility corrections to match the full-score configuration's exact bytes; they are not unconstrained greedy rules. At each budget, the three rules occupy identical static bytes despite possibly different protected-layer counts.

In the order full-policy score / final-action error / largest first, GR00T achieves 51.6/52.2/52.2\% at 900,268,032 bytes (95\% initializer ceiling) and 54.4/53.8/53.8\% at 855,048,192 bytes (90\% ceiling). \paragraph{Inference and scope.}
All eight planned exact two-sided McNemar comparisons have raw $p\ge0.504$ and Holm-adjusted $p=1.0$. All eight task-clustered bootstrap descriptive 95\% success-difference intervals include zero. These results do not establish a stable success advantage for full-score deletion, equivalence of the rules, or absence of small effects. The study permits only removal of initial protections and omits later candidate-state admission, so it is a projection-rule comparison rather than an ablation of all FCP stages. Newly generated $\pi_{0.5}$ masks use corrected statistics: matching a historical layer count and byte size does not establish mask identity, and these results do not replace Table~\ref{tab:main_results}.

\subsection{Independent Static-A8 Coverage Confirmation}
\label{sec:static_a8_confirmation}

\paragraph{Design and frozen controls.}
This follow-up evaluates four activation-range rules on all 50 RoboCasa365 tasks with ten new seeds, 23000--23009, per task: 500 episodes per arm and 2,000 in total. Its protocol was frozen after an exploratory 18-task/two-seed pilot but before these new outcomes were collected. Runtime audits confirm the shared plan, weights, checkpoint, wrapped-layer count, and each arm's activation table. All 12 split--arm cells complete without error rows.

\paragraph{What changes between the static controls.}
The registered arm reuses the historical frozen activation table. Two additional static tables are recalibrated from the original FP16 checkpoint separately for each split, using the same 256 calibration observations and paired noise, batch size eight, and 99.9th-percentile scaling per input channel and applicable flow step. The pooled table computes the quantile over all captured rows; the sparse table retains only the first and last flattened row of each forward capture. Thus the dense--sparse comparison changes sampling coverage within the same calibration observations, not the calibration source or number of observations. Both tables remain frozen during evaluation. The label FP16 describes their calibration source; all evaluated policies retain the same mixed W4/native weight layout. The fourth arm uses input-conditioned \dyrange.

\paragraph{Primary coverage result.}
Dense static calibration obtains 216/500 successes (43.2\%), whereas the matched two-row table and the historical registered table each obtain 0/500. For the pre-frozen primary dense-minus-sparse contrast, there are 216 paired wins, zero losses, and 284 ties: a 43.2-point difference, task-cluster bootstrap 95\% CI $[35.6,51.0]$ points, and exact two-sided McNemar $p=1.90\times10^{-65}$ (Holm-adjusted $p=2.28\times10^{-64}$). The dense-minus-registered comparison has the same counts and statistics. The matched dense--sparse intervention demonstrates that sparse calibration sampling can cause collapse in this setting, while a frozen range table with broader captured-row coverage can recover substantial success.

\paragraph{Dynamic ranges against the stronger static control.}
Dynamic A8 obtains 269/500 successes (53.8\%), with 106 paired wins, 53 losses, and 341 ties against dense static A8. The 10.6-point advantage has 95\% CI $[6.2,15.0]$ points, exact McNemar $p=3.18\times10^{-5}$, and Holm $p=1.27\times10^{-4}$. All 12 pre-frozen comparisons (three contrasts across the overall set and three splits) share one Holm family. Dynamic-minus-dense split differences are 12.8, 10.6, and 8.1 points, with adjusted $p=0.0155$, $0.0540$, and $0.0660$, respectively; the latter two are not significant at 0.05. The 95\% intervals use 20,000 task-cluster bootstrap replicates with RNG seed 20260907 and are pointwise, not simultaneous family-adjusted intervals.

They establish neither failure of all static schemes nor a separate causal attribution of the remaining dynamic advantage to policy-induced state shift. The new dynamic aggregate happens to equal the historical 269/500 result, but the task--seed records and split outcomes differ and are not pooled. \subsection{FCP Projection, Adjudication, and Freeze Rules}
\label{sec:fcp_rules}

\paragraph{Evidence-guided feasibility projection.}
If $C(M_{\mathrm{init}})\le C_{\max}$, FCP sets $M_0=M_{\mathrm{init}}$. Otherwise, it instantiates each FP16-to-W4 removal as a complete policy and ranks removals by their deviation evidence. For shared context $\mathcal B$, a coordinate intervention has the form
\begin{equation}
 \Delta_i(M)=D(Q_{M^{(i)}};\mathcal B)-D(Q_M;\mathcal B),
 \label{eq:full_policy_intervention}
\end{equation}
where $M^{(i)}$ changes target $i$ and $D$ denotes the applicable full-policy deviation criterion. The intervention is measured in the current mixed-precision background rather than as isolated layer reconstruction, and protections are removed from least to most damaging until the mask is feasible. This projection uses ranked single-layer evidence, not an exact solution of all multi-layer interactions.

\paragraph{Coordinate counterfactuals and the candidate class.}
Starting from $M_0$, FCP flips target precision states one at a time and, when scheduled, repeats the sweep around the preceding winner. Each intervention is instantiated as a mixed-precision network and compared with its complete-mask background, and the resulting conservative benefits feed an exact sparse Pareto 0--1 dynamic program subject to
\begin{equation}
 C(M)=C_{\mathrm{fixed}}+\sum_{i\in\mathcal{L}}C_i(M_i)
 \le 1.10\,C_{\mathrm{QuantVLA}}.
 \label{eq:budget}
\end{equation}
The constraint is expressed in bytes rather than layer count because layers differ in tensor and metadata size. The dynamic program only generates candidates: its additive benefits cannot capture interactions among simultaneous layer changes. The finite class $\mathcal C(M_0)$ contains the anchor, dynamic-program proposals, and five structured proposal types rebuilt from layer scores, and full-policy evaluation checks these complete configurations.

\paragraph{Task-level minimax adjudication.}
For metric $q\in\{\dfunc,\dpac\}$ and task cluster $g\in\{\mathrm{all},\mathrm{Atomic},\mathrm{C\text{-}Seen},\mathrm{C\text{-}Unseen}\}$, let $\Delta_{q,t}(M)$ be the paired candidate-minus-$M_0$ task scalar. We define
\begin{equation}
 U_{q,g}(M)=
 \frac{\operatorname{mean}_{t\in g}\Delta_{q,t}(M)+
 \operatorname{SE}^{\mathrm{jack}}_{t\in g}[\Delta_{q,t}(M)]}
 {\operatorname{mean}_{t\in g}q_t(M_{\mathrm{base}})},
 \qquad
 J(M)=\max_{q,g}U_{q,g}(M).
 \label{eq:minimax}
\end{equation}
Here $M_{\mathrm{base}}=M_0$ shares the candidate's context, and the jackknife uses tasks as uncertainty units. With positive denominators, $J(M)<0$ requires \emph{strict one-SE improvement in every metric--group pair}, plus non-positive component bounds for pose, stitch, and gripper. The rule preserves the anchor unless deviation evidence favors replacement: extra compression alone cannot justify admission. It is frozen after evaluation and supplies no safety or success guarantee.

\paragraph{Candidate-state check and freeze.}
The protocol reserves candidate-induced observations over four to eight replans and defines $J_{\mathrm{final}}=\max(J_{\mathrm{teacher}},J_{\mathrm{candidate}})$. Eligible teacher-state candidates are first filtered by a paired one-standard-error comparison against the best teacher-state objective; up to three then enter candidate-state adjudication. Let $\mathcal E(M_0)$ denote the remaining byte-feasible candidates with negative $J_{\mathrm{final}}$ and the required deviation guards. The frozen choice is
\begin{equation}
 \widehat M=\begin{cases}
 \operatorname*{lexmin}_{M\in\mathcal E(M_0)}
  (C(M),N_{16}(M),\operatorname{names}(M)),&\mathcal E(M_0)\ne\varnothing,\\
 M_0,&\mathcal E(M_0)=\varnothing,
 \end{cases}
 \label{eq:restricted_freeze}
\end{equation}
where $N_{16}$ counts protected layers and names break ties deterministically. Storage is compared only after deviation eligibility. On GR00T all proposals fail the teacher-state screen, so FCP retains $M_0$ without candidate-state evaluation or any success-optimality claim.

\subsection{Exact-Budget Offline Proxy Predictive Validity}
\label{sec:predictive_validity}

As an auxiliary diagnostic, we test whether frozen offline scores rank the closed-loop consequences of precision allocation. The library contains 60 masks with identical 100-W4/16-FP16 allocation and exact static bytes, stratified into ten masks at each of six swap distances from $M_0$. We freeze offline scores before running one rollout per target task and mask. Across all masks, Spearman correlation with signed success degradation is $-0.116$ for \dpac and $-0.028$ for MSE. Mask distance confounds this pooled comparison: larger swaps have greater offline distortion but, in this library, slightly higher mean success. Within each fixed-distance stratum, the registered \dpac correlations are positive but imprecise ($\rho=0.055$--$0.352$, ten masks per stratum), and \dpac does not consistently outperform MSE or \dfunc. These results position \dpac as a predeclared complete-policy deviation guard rather than a success-ranking objective.
